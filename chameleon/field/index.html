Chameleon Ultra field probe (experimental)

Point the CU at a reader instead of a card, and find out what kind of field the reader makes. On LF, the CU measures the carrier's frequency and looks for the short gaps a reader leaves in its field when it sends a command. On HF, it counts the ISO 14443-A polls the reader sends. The CU logs each field, so you can take it to a reader on battery and read the results here afterwards.

Everything happens in this browser. The page talks to the CU over USB. Nothing is sent to any server, and it doesn't save anything unless you press Copy or Download.

While the probe is on, the CU doesn't emulate any card. No slot answers a reader. Turn the probe off here when you're done, or flash stock firmware.

Connect the Chameleon Ultra

This page requires Chrome or Edge on a computer. Use USB, not Bluetooth, and close anything else using the CU, such as the CU app or the Python CLI.

Checking the firmware…

This firmware has no field probe. Flash the experimental firmware below, then connect again.

1. Turn the probe on

The probe stays on through sleep, unplugging and reconnecting, until you turn it off. It goes off if the battery runs flat.

2. Take it to a reader

Unplug the CU and hold its button side flat against the reader for about 5 seconds, then take it away. The field LED and a cyan light show an LF field being measured; green shows an HF field. You can visit more than one reader, but the log keeps only the last 12 LF measurements, and each reader's field is easier to read on its own.

With the CU still plugged in, you can hold it against a reader and press Measure now instead. That measures LF once, straight away.

3. Read the results

Plug the CU back in, connect, and read the log. Clear it before measuring a different reader.

Advanced: envelope mode and raw captures

The envelope capture looks for the reader's gaps. With the load on (the default), the CU's detector follows the field quickly. With it off, the detector holds the field's level and short gaps don't show.

A raw capture records LF_RSSI with the CU held against a reader, for analysis elsewhere. The samples can include a reader's commands, so treat the download as private.

Firmware

The probe needs the experimental firmware: the card search page's firmware (lf-search) with the field probe added (source). Flashing replaces the CU's firmware and keeps the current slots.

What the page does

        
    
          
  1. 
            Turn the probe on sets the CU to tag mode and switches the
            probe on. From then on the CU senses LF and HF fields whatever its
            slots hold, and never answers.
          
    2. 
          
  2. 
            When an LF field appears, the CU measures it at the start,
            after 1 s and after 4 s. Each measurement first records 102 ms of
            the field's envelope at 100 kHz, while a reader that sends one
            command may still be sending, and finds every dip in it. The
            carrier's ripple in that envelope gives the reader's carrier
            frequency. Then it records the field's level with the CU's load
            off.
          
    3. 
          
  3. 
            When an HF field appears, the CU listens with its ISO
            14443-A receiver and counts what the reader sends: polls (REQA and
            WUPA), other frames and frames received with errors.
          
    4. 
          
  4. 
            Read the log reads the CU's log and works out, in this
            browser, what the measurements say.
          
    5. 
        

        

          The carrier frequency is measured against the CU's own crystal, which
          can be off by up to ±40 ppm. A steady LF field means the reader waits
          for a card to talk (EM4100, HID Prox, Indala, Paradox). Gaps in the
          field mean the reader sends commands, as Hitag readers and readers of
          password-protected T55xx or EM4x05 tags do. The CU's HF receiver only
          decodes ISO 14443-A, so a reader of iCLASS, ISO 15693 or FeliCa cards
          shows as a field with no frames.
        

        

          It has been checked against a Proxmark3, not yet against door
          readers. The numbers are a guide, not a verdict.
